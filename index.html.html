Pillars of the Mindful City

Four defining structures that anchor the spiritual, civic, and connective life of Gelephu Mindfulness City.

ChoeGo GMC

The spiritual nucleus of GMC — a landmark blending traditional Bhutanese sacred architecture with the language of tomorrow's cities.

02 — Healthcare

GMC Hospital

A world-class wellness and medical facility designed to integrate modern healthcare with the healing principles of mindfulness and traditional medicine.

// The Human Force

100K

The Power of Zhabtog

Zhabtog — the Bhutanese tradition of voluntary service — is the beating heart of GMC's construction. Thousands of citizens gave their labor, sweat, and spirit to build something that will last centuries.

"When a people build together, they build more than structures — they build a nation's soul."

🏔️

Unity in Labor

Citizens from all 20 dzongkhags contributed Zhabtog shifts — a modern expression of age-old collective spirit that has shaped Bhutan for centuries.

🌱

Ecological Mindfulness

Every volunteer effort was guided by GMC's core principle: zero compromise on nature. Over 60% of the city area remains forested and biodiverse.

⚡

Youth Vanguard

Bhutan's youth — from schools, colleges, and monasteries — led the Zhabtog brigades, planting the seeds of ownership in the city's foundation itself.

// Design & Masterplan

The Master Planners

The visionary architectural minds shaping GMC's physical reality — marrying Bhutanese identity with global best practice.

🏛️

BIG Architects

Bjarke Ingels Group — the world-renowned Danish firm known for projects like The LEGO House, 2 World Trade Center, and Vancouver's Telus Sky — was commissioned to master-plan GMC's urban fabric. Their human-scale, nature-integrated approach perfectly aligns with the mindfulness ethos of the city.

MASTERPLAN LEAD
🌏

Pelsung Architecture

Pelsung brings the irreplaceable soul of Bhutanese architectural tradition into every structure. Their deep expertise in dzong-style sacred forms, timber joinery, and landscape integration ensures GMC is unmistakably and proudly Bhutanese — even as it reaches for the future.
